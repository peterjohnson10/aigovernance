The AI Act Readiness Scan

A twenty-minute self-assessment for European small and medium enterprises. Find out what the EU AI Act already requires of you, what it will require next, and where your accountability gap sits today.

Why this matters now

The clock has already started running

Most European SMEs believe the AI Act is a 2027 problem. Three sets of obligations say otherwise, and one of them lands on 2 August 2026.

The Digital Omnibus on AI, adopted by the European Parliament on 16 June 2026 and the Council on 29 June 2026, entered into force in July 2026. It deferred the heavy high-risk obligations. It did not defer the obligations that touch almost every business using a chatbot, a generative writing tool, or an image generator. As Freshfields put it in its analysis of the final text, businesses gain "a meaningful runway but that time should be used productively as the underlying obligations have not changed."

  • 2 Feb 2025Prohibited AI practices and the Article 4 AI literacy duty became enforceable.Live
  • 2 Aug 2025Obligations for providers of general-purpose AI models, plus the governance architecture.Live
  • 2 Aug 2026Article 50 transparency duties apply. National market surveillance authorities and the Commission gain enforcement powers.Imminent
  • 2 Dec 2026Grace period ends for machine-readable marking of AI-generated content on systems placed on the market before 2 August 2026. New prohibition on nudification and CSAM generators takes effect.Imminent
  • 2 Aug 2027Deadline for Member States to establish national AI regulatory sandboxes.Horizon
  • 2 Dec 2027Obligations for standalone high-risk systems listed in Annex III: employment, credit, education, biometrics, essential services.Horizon
  • 2 Aug 2028Obligations for high-risk AI embedded in products covered by Annex I product safety law.Horizon

What this scan does

Five questions, honestly answered

Every credible AI governance programme, in a multinational bank or a forty-person engineering firm, answers the same five questions. This scan walks you through them and scores your answers.

One. What AI is actually running inside your business? Two. Are you a provider, a deployer, or both? Three. What risk tier does each system sit in? Four. What must be in place, and by when? Five. Who is accountable for each system, and could they prove it to a regulator on a Tuesday morning?

The distance between the decisions your AI is already making and the named person who can explain, defend and correct them is your accountability gap. It is the single most useful thing to measure, because it predicts everything else: whether you will pass an audit, whether you will catch an error before a customer does, and whether your insurer and your bank will treat you as a serious counterparty.

Your report is generated entirely in your browser. Data leaves this page in only two cases, both optional and both explained below: if you contribute your anonymous scores to the readiness benchmark, or if you leave contact details for follow-up. There are no cookies and no tracking. Print or save this page when you finish to keep your result.

Part A

Your position under the Act

Size determines which reliefs you can claim. The AI Act gives real, concrete advantages to smaller companies, and the Digital Omnibus extended several of them to the small mid-cap tier that captures much of the German Mittelstand.

SME thresholds come from Recommendation 2003/361/EC. The small mid-cap category was created by Commission Recommendation (EU) 2025/1099.
This determines which sectoral regime sits on top of the AI Act.
National supervisory structures differ sharply. Some Member States are enforcement-ready; most are not yet.
Do you put your own name or brand on an AI system, or materially modify one before it reaches users? This is the provider test. White-labelling a chatbot, fine-tuning a model and shipping it, or embedding AI in a product you sell all make you a provider, with a far heavier obligation set than a deployer.

Part B

The stop line

Eight practices have been prohibited outright since February 2025, with a ninth added by the Digital Omnibus. These are not risk-managed. They are banned, and they carry the heaviest fines in the Act. Tick anything your business does, or is considering.

Part C

What is actually running

Tick every AI system in use anywhere in the business, including tools a single department bought on a card without telling anyone. Shadow AI is the most common finding in a first inventory and the most common source of an unpleasant surprise.

Part D

The six-domain readiness diagnostic

Eighteen questions, three per domain. Answer for the business as it actually operates today, not as the policy says it should. Optimism bias is the main reason first self-assessments are wrong, and the main reason the second one hurts.

Part E

Contribute to the readiness benchmark

There is no reliable public data on how prepared European SMEs actually are for the AI Act. Almost every published figure describes large enterprises. This tool is building the missing benchmark, and the results will be published openly so that companies your size can see where they stand against their peers.

Exactly what is sent, and nothing else: your size band, sector, country, the six domain scores, your total score, which risk categories your systems fall into, and which prohibited practices you flagged.
